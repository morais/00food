# 00Food for iOS

**Your food log. Your AI agent.** The sign-in screen introduces the bring-your-own-agent workflow. After profile setup, accounts without an agent see a prominent Connect your agent card. The same guide is available in Settings, recommends ChatGPT Work, and provides copyable MCP connection and event-monitoring instructions. It shows authorized connections separately from their currently unexpired event subscriptions. New-food estimates remain subject to user review; manual logging remains available. See [agent setup](../docs/agent-setup.md).

SwiftUI app for iPhone, built with Xcode 27 and XcodeGen. The app and widget target iPhone only (`TARGETED_DEVICE_FAMILY = 1`); Mac Catalyst, Mac compatibility destinations, and Apple Vision compatibility destinations are disabled. TestFlight groups also disable Apple silicon Mac and Apple Vision availability. Native iPad support and iPad App Store screenshots are removed; iOS may still permit installation in iPhone compatibility mode on iPad. The main screen shows today's rough balance, pending agent estimates, and today's entries. Home Screen quick actions open Log food or open Log food directly into the camera. Log food prioritizes saved foods for one-tap reuse and has no built-in starter list. Unfamiliar foods can be saved with a manual estimate or sent with a description, a photo, or both to a remote MCP agent for review. The new-food form keeps its wrapping description field (growing up to six lines) and attached-photo preview in one section, with Photos and Camera side by side. Manual entry is a separate section. The description and photo are submitted atomically under the same pending-food ID so the agent can consider both.

After the first successful sign-in and sync, the app opens from an on-device snapshot without a connection. Saved foods, manual foods, logs, deletions, profile edits, and new estimate requests (including their photos) are saved locally and replayed in order when the connection returns. The original meal date and time are preserved. A status strip shows queued changes and sync errors. Sign-out waits until queued changes have synced, so it cannot silently discard them. Initial Apple sign-in, agent estimation and review, account deletion, and MCP connection management require a connection. Apple Health reads work on device; the widget uses the latest local app snapshot.

Empty Health history is normal, while real read failures preserve the previous resting average. The app restores the latest successful active-energy, resting-energy, weight/body-fat, and water readings from a local display cache keyed by day and timezone. Cached readings stay visible during refresh; the budget updates together after a successful read, and water updates independently. Yesterday’s cache is never used for today, and feedback requests still read Health directly. With no current-day budget, the pace marker stays neutral with “Updating allowance…” until the first successful Health read. Your details & Health can write a manual weight sample to Health and save the value to the 00Food profile. The daily budget uses the average resting energy from at least five of the last seven completed days; it falls back to the details-based estimate when that history is unavailable. Your details & Health shows both resting-energy values, with their explanation and difference behind info buttons. Onboarding requests Dietary Energy write access. New logs are exported automatically as `dietaryEnergyConsumed` when allowed; declined access does not block logging. Exports are keyed by account and log ID; deleting a log deletes its matching Health sample. Earlier logs are not backfilled. Health energy and historical measurements stay on device by default. If Daily feedback is enabled in Settings, the app automatically uploads up to seven completed days of available Health daily aggregates to the private 00Food account, with an offline queue, on the next open or after midnight while active. With daily feedback enabled, it also requests an on-device background refresh after 00:15 local time through BGAppRefreshTask. iOS chooses the actual execution time. Locked Health data or failed sync defers the request and schedules a retry at least an hour later; completed runs schedule the next local 00:15, accounting for timezone and daylight-saving changes. Background refresh submits the same MCP event as foreground requests, and the connected agent still produces the review. No server midnight scheduler is used. ## Agent response checks

Body-fat history uses the last recorded Health sample in each local calendar day, retaining its timestamp. The latest chart dot and illustration start therefore match the latest value shown elsewhere. Daily-review Health summaries use the same per-day body-fat selection. Reviews render headings, lists, inline emphasis and web links with Foundation's Markdown parser and native SwiftUI text.

Pending food estimates, clarification replies, and daily reviews refresh automatically while the iPhone app is active and online: every 15 seconds for two minutes, then every minute. Returning to the app checks immediately. The loop stops after all replies arrive, on sign-out, offline, or in the background. These checks reuse the conditional snapshot request, preserve cached content, and do not query Health or show polling errors.

Agent replies also queue a silent push to registered iPhones. This requests a background snapshot refresh with a 20-second limit and no Health reads. iOS decides whether and when to run it; foreground checks remain the fallback. Registration requires a signed-in account, renews every 12 hours while active, and is removed on sign-out. Release builds use production APNs; Debug registration remains disabled unless a separate development key is configured. The app needs Push Notifications, a matching distribution profile, and the remote-notification background mode. Open a new TestFlight build once to register the device; no alert permission prompt is needed.

## Apple Watch

New accounts first see the Health read-permission explanation, then a minimal setup prefilled with readable Health height, weight, and biological sex. Height and the optional estimate setting remain editable; missing height or weight can be entered manually. The Developer page can queue onboarding for the next process launch; replay preserves the selected plan and can be cancelled without saving. Health write permissions are requested when their corresponding actions are used. Saved-food search starts hidden and is revealed by pulling down past the top of the list. It stays inside the list, works with short lists, and can be cleared and hidden again. Watch confirmations and Undo float near the top so the food and water buttons keep their positions.

The companion Watch app requires watchOS 11 or later and uses the account set up on the paired iPhone. Today shows calories left, eaten/active energy, the same two-point day pace, water glasses, and up to five fruit/veg portions. Log food lists the 50 most-used visible saved foods; tap to log one serving, or adjust the portion. New food uses the Watch's native text entry (including dictation) and queues a text-only request for the existing MCP agent. Pending requests and proposals show their status; estimates are reviewed on iPhone. Water adds 250 mL with an Undo action. The circular complication shows calories left; the rectangular complication/Smart Stack widget also opens food and water logging.

The Watch balance screen has no app title and uses compact text buttons to fit smaller displays. Successful food and water logs return to the balance screen, where their confirmation and Undo remain available.

Watch actions save to an atomic, protected local queue before showing confirmation. WatchConnectivity transfers them to the iPhone, which stages food/estimate changes in its existing offline queue and writes water to Health. No app token is copied to Watch. Original log timestamps and local dates are preserved. Retries use the same action/log ID; water samples have matching Health sync identifiers. A durable phone inbox deduplicates foreground and background deliveries, and an Undo tombstone prevents late deliveries from restoring cancelled actions. Account checks stop a queued action from entering another account. Snapshots acknowledge saved actions, include log IDs to avoid double-counting optimistic entries, reject older updates, and expire at the local day/timezone boundary. Water permission must first be enabled by logging water on iPhone. Queued changes need the paired iPhone to complete; complications show cached readings and refresh when new state arrives.

Generate both app targets with `xcodegen generate`. Build the `ZeroZeroFoodWatch` scheme for Watch simulator UI checks; archive `ZeroZeroFood` to include Watch and its widgets in the iPhone TestFlight build. Register matching Watch app and widget bundle IDs and associate the existing App Group with both. For manual distribution signing, use separate App Store profiles for all four targets (iPhone app, iPhone widgets, Watch app, and Watch widgets), set each target's Release profile in the local project file, and include all four bundle IDs in the export options. Verify the Watch profiles contain the App Group entitlement before archiving. WatchConnectivity background transfers must also be tested on a physical paired iPhone/Watch; the simulator does not support the background `transferUserInfo` path. The shared queue/reconciliation behavior is covered by `WatchSyncTests`.

## Missing food from a past day

The bottom of each past-day food list has Add missing food. The normal saved-food, manual-entry, and photo/description estimate flows carry that day through the offline queue, acceptance, and Dietary Energy export. Log creation time is stored separately from meal time, so a new backdated entry is exported without backfilling old logs.

When a food is added to a day with an existing review and automatic daily reviews are enabled, Request new review appears below the list. It refreshes that day’s Health context and queues a new agent event only on request. The replacement has a new request ID; retries are idempotent and a response to the superseded request cannot overwrite the new review. Past-day water shows a checkmark at eight glasses or more, matching the capped produce goal.

## Forbes body-composition forecasts

When Apple Health supplies body fat (including connected scales), forecasts use fat mass = weight × body-fat fraction and an iterative fat-loss share of FM / (FM + 10.4). Small mass steps recalculate that share as fat mass falls; remaining fat-free mass is weight minus fat mass. Fat percentages and ACE target dates use the same model, and pace previews show the projected fat and fat-free mass split. With no body-fat reading, there is no body-composition forecast or invented starting percentage.

Each refresh recalibrates from a trailing seven-calendar-day median, counting one reading per local day. Less history uses available readings. Observed daily weight drops are not converted into fat loss; the model partitions only the plan’s projected sustained change. Smoothing dampens short-term noise but cannot identify water or glycogen changes separately. The underlying directional weight forecast still uses about 7,700 kcal/kg and fixed recent TDEE.

Model reference: [Hall, Body fat and fat-free mass interrelationships: Forbes’s theory revisited (2007)](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0007114507691946).
